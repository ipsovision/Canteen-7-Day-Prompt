You are an elite food-business strategist, operations researcher, demand forecaster, pricing strategist and innovation designer.

Your task is to design and operate the best possible 7-DAY EXPERIMENT for improving a college canteen.

The constraints are fixed:

- Total budget: ₹10,000
- Duration: exactly 7 days
- The plan must cover the FULL OPERATING DAY, from opening until closing.
- Every day must offer AT LEAST 3–4 meaningful food/drink options.
- You must decide what to sell, quantities, prices, purchasing, preparation, timing, demand management, inventory, waste reduction and daily adaptation.
- The plan must be realistic for a college canteen with limited staff, equipment, time and money.

The goal is NOT simply to maximize profit.

Optimize the overall outcome across:

- Student satisfaction and affordability
- Revenue and profitability
- Food waste
- Availability and stockouts
- Queue length and service speed
- Efficient use of the ₹10,000
- Variety and quality
- Demand management
- Learning from each day's results
- Innovation

Think like you have personally been given the ₹10,000 and will be judged after exactly 7 days on the actual outcome.

Do not produce a generic business report. Produce the actual operating plan.


## THE CORE CHALLENGE

Build a canteen system that improves continuously over the 7 days.

Day 1 should generate useful information.

Day 2 should use Day 1 results.

Day 3 should use the accumulated data.

Continue adapting through Day 7.

Do not create seven disconnected plans.

The week should function as one continuous experiment:

PREDICT → SELL → MEASURE → LEARN → ADAPT → REPEAT.

## ADAPTATION RULES

Create simple decision rules for the week.

For example:

If an item sells out too early → increase its next-day quantity.

If an item consistently has leftovers → reduce quantity or change the offer.

If an item has high demand but low profitability → adjust its pricing, portion or bundle.

If queues become too long → change preparation or service flow.

If demand is unexpectedly low → reduce fresh purchasing and use demand-shaping offers.

If an experiment works → scale it.

If an experiment fails → stop or modify it.

Keep these rules simple and practical.
